FICA Tip Credit Calculator: What It Is and Who Qualifies

The FICA tip credit gives food and beverage employers back part of the payroll tax they pay on employee tips. This page explains who can claim it and which tips count. When you are ready for numbers, use the Form 8846 calculator.

What the FICA Tip Credit Is

Employers pay 7.65% in Social Security and Medicare tax on wages, including the tips employees report. Section 45B of the tax code lets certain employers claim a credit for part of that tax on tips. You claim it on Form 8846, and it counts as part of the general business credit.

This is not a refund of tax withheld from your employees. It lowers your own income tax bill. If you cannot use all of it in one year, the unused amount can be carried to other years under the general business credit rules: generally a 1-year carryback and a 20-year carryforward under IRC §39. Confirm the current rule with your tax preparer before relying on it.

Who Qualifies

The business

Your employees must provide, deliver, or serve food or drinks, and customers must customarily tip for that service. Restaurants, bars, cafes, and catering or delivery operations can qualify on that basis. Partnerships and S corporations can qualify too, and the credit flows through to the owners.

The workers

The credit is built on employee tips. Workers you correctly treat as independent contractors do not count, because you do not pay employer FICA on their pay.

The tax paid

Employees must report tips to you, and you must have paid or incurred employer FICA tax on them. A tip that was never reported, and never taxed, is outside the credit.

What the Credit Does on Your Return

  • It lowers income tax. The credit is nonrefundable. It cannot push your tax below zero.
  • It is claimed on Form 3800. Form 8846 feeds the General Business Credit. Pass-through owners receive their share on a Schedule K-1.
  • It trims your deduction. You cannot deduct the same amount as payroll expense.
  • It does not change your deposits. You still withhold, deposit, and report FICA on Form 941 as usual. The credit shows up only on the income tax return.

How to Estimate Your Credit

For each tipped employee, you need four numbers: reported tips, hours worked, cash wages paid, and wages plus tips for the year (to check the wage base). Payroll reports usually have all four. Box 7 of the W-2 shows Social Security tips, and Form 941 totals them by quarter. Large establishments that file Form 8027 can use it to cross-check tip totals.

FAQ

Is the FICA tip credit the same as the tip credit that lets me pay a lower cash wage?

No. The wage-law tip credit lets an employer count tips toward the minimum wage and pay a lower cash wage. The FICA tip credit is a federal income tax credit for the employer payroll tax paid on tips. They are separate rules with separate purposes.

Can I claim the credit if I use a payroll provider?

Yes. The provider handles payroll tax filings, but you or your tax preparer claim the credit on your income tax return. Ask the provider for a report of tips, hours, and cash wages by employee.

Can a sole proprietor claim the credit?

Yes. A sole proprietor files Form 8846 with the individual return and carries the credit to Form 3800.

Does claiming the credit change what I deposit on Form 941?

No. Keep depositing and reporting FICA taxes the same way. The credit is claimed later, on your income tax return.
