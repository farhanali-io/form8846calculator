Restaurant Tip Credit Calculator: How Food and Beverage Employers Claim Form 8846

Restaurants, bars, and similar food-service businesses are the core users of the FICA tip credit. The statute was written around tips for providing, delivering, or serving food and drinks. This page shows how the credit works inside a restaurant, which roles generate creditable tips, and how tip pools and service charges affect the Form 8846 numbers.

Why Restaurants Are the Main Users of Form 8846

Section 45B limits the credit to tips received for food and beverage service where tipping is customary. That description matches full-service restaurants, bars, cafes, caterers, and many delivery operations. Other tipped businesses generally fall outside the credit. Because restaurants already report tips for payroll-tax purposes, the same data feeds Form 8846 with little extra record-keeping.

The credit does not change how you withhold or deposit FICA. It only appears later on the income-tax return. For the eligibility rules that apply beyond restaurants, see the FICA tip credit guide.

Which Restaurant Roles Generate Creditable Tips

Tips count when they are paid to employees for food or beverage service and employer FICA is paid on them. Typical roles that generate creditable tips include:

  • Servers and waitstaff
  • Bartenders
  • Bussers and food runners who share in a tip pool
  • Delivery drivers employed by the restaurant (not by a third-party platform)

Hosts, managers, and supervisors usually do not receive tips. If they do receive tips and are correctly treated as employees with FICA paid, those tips can still enter the calculation. Independent contractors never generate a credit because the restaurant does not pay employer FICA on their earnings.

Tip Pooling and Form 8846

Many restaurants pool tips and then distribute shares to servers, bartenders, and support staff. For Form 8846 the credit is based on each employee's own reported tips after the pool is allocated, not on the pool total before distribution. The employee who ultimately reports the tip on the payroll records is the one whose hours and cash wages are used for the Line 2 exclusion.

Keep the post-pool tip reports. Those are the numbers that match Box 7 of the W-2 and the tip lines on Form 941, and they are the numbers that belong on Line 1 of Form 8846.

Service Charges vs Tips

A mandatory service charge or automatic gratuity that the customer cannot decline is generally treated as a wage, not a tip. Because it is not a tip, it does not enter the Form 8846 calculation and does not generate a credit. True tips are voluntary amounts the customer chooses to leave. When a check includes both a mandatory service charge and a voluntary tip, only the voluntary tip counts for the credit.

Payroll systems sometimes label both items the same way. Review the POS configuration and the tip reports so that only voluntary tips reach Line 1.

Delivery and Takeout Tips

Tips for "providing, delivering, or serving food or drinks" qualify when the restaurant is the employer. Tips paid to a restaurant's own delivery drivers therefore usually count. Tips left through a third-party delivery app generally do not, because the platform—not the restaurant—is the employer of record and the one that pays (or does not pay) employer FICA on those tips.

If your restaurant employs its own drivers and reports their tips for FICA purposes, include those tips in the Form 8846 calculation under the same rules that apply to floor staff.

FAQ

Do bartenders' tips count the same way as servers' tips?

Yes. Both are tips for food or beverage service. The same $5.15 floor, the same reporting requirements, and the same 7.65% rate apply.

Can I claim the credit on tips that were never reported?

No. Only tips on which you paid or incurred employer FICA tax are eligible. Unreported tips never enter the calculation.

Does tip pooling reduce the credit?

Not by itself. The credit follows the tips each employee reports after the pool is allocated. The pool total is irrelevant; the individual reported amounts matter.

Where can I see the cash-wage rules that apply in my state?

See the tip credit rules by state page. State law sets the cash wage you pay; the federal $5.15 floor used for the Form 8846 exclusion stays the same.
