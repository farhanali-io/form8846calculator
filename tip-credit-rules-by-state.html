Tip Credit Rules by State

This page is a reference table of tipped cash wages and tip-credit rules across the 50 states and the District of Columbia. It is not tax, legal, or payroll advice. State tip-credit rules are wage-and-hour law. The FICA tip credit on Form 8846 is a separate federal income-tax credit. State rules affect only the cash wage you pay (and therefore the cash-wage number you enter when you figure Line 2 of Form 8846). They do not change the federal $5.15 floor used for the credit itself.

How State Tip Credit Rules Affect Form 8846

Under federal labor law an employer may pay a tipped employee a cash wage as low as $2.13 an hour and count tips toward the $7.25 federal minimum. Many states raise that cash floor or ban the tip credit entirely. The higher the cash wage you actually pay, the smaller (or zero) the exclusion on Form 8846 Line 2, and the larger the potential FICA tip credit. See the $5.15 rule explainer for why the federal floor never moves.

The Most Complex States

California bans the tip credit. Employers pay the full state minimum ($16.90 in 2026) before tips. Local and industry rates (fast food, health care) can be higher. Because cash wages already exceed $5.15, Form 8846 Line 2 is normally zero.

New York sets different cash wages by region and by industry. Hospitality rates differ between New York City / Long Island / Westchester and the rest of the state; many non-hospitality tipped occupations must receive the full minimum. Confirm the exact schedule that applies to your establishment.

Alaska, Oregon, Washington, Minnesota, Montana, and Nevada also prohibit the tip credit. Cash wages equal the full applicable state (or regional) minimum. Oregon's three regional rates change every July 1.

Several other states index their rates annually or have pending legislation. Always re-check the current notice before the start of a new calendar year.

States With No Tip Credit

Seven states require the full applicable minimum wage in cash before tips:

  • Alaska
  • California
  • Minnesota
  • Montana
  • Nevada
  • Oregon
  • Washington

In these states the cash wage already exceeds the federal $5.15 floor used on Form 8846. Line 2 is typically zero, so nearly all reported tips become creditable and the FICA tip credit is larger than it would be under a low cash-wage regime.

How to Use This Table With the Calculator

  1. Find your state (and region, if applicable) in the table above and note the tipped cash wage that applies to your employees.
  2. Gather each tipped employee's reported tips, hours worked, and actual cash wages paid for the period you are calculating.
  3. Enter those figures in the Form 8846 calculator. The cash-wage amount you enter drives the Line 2 exclusion; the $5.15 federal floor stays fixed.

FAQ

My state has no tip credit. Do I still qualify for the FICA tip credit?

Yes. The two rules are independent. A state that bans the wage-law tip credit simply forces a higher cash wage. That higher cash wage usually produces a larger Form 8846 credit because fewer (or zero) tips are excluded on Line 2.

Does a local minimum wage override the state tipped cash wage?

Often yes. Many cities and counties set higher floors. Use the higher of the state and local rate that applies to your location.

Where do I find the official DOL table?

The U.S. Department of Labor publishes the national summary at dol.gov under "Minimum Wages for Tipped Employees." State labor departments publish the controlling local notices.

Do these cash wages change the $5.15 figure on Form 8846?

No. The $5.15 floor is fixed by IRC §45B. State cash wages only change the number you subtract when you compute the exclusion. See the $5.15 rule page.
