Tip Credit vs Payroll Tax Credit: What's the Difference?

"Tip credit" means at least three different things depending on who is speaking. Confusing them produces the wrong form, the wrong year, and the wrong expectation about cash flow. This page separates the concepts so you can see which one Form 8846 actually is.

Three Different "Tip Credits"

  1. The wage-law tip credit. Under the Fair Labor Standards Act and parallel state laws, an employer may pay a tipped employee a cash wage below the full minimum and count tips toward the remainder. This is a labor-law rule, not a tax credit.
  2. The FICA tip credit (Form 8846). A federal income-tax credit equal to the employer's 7.65% Social Security and Medicare tax on certain reported tips. It appears on the income-tax return, not on Form 941.
  3. Informal use for employee-side credits. Some people loosely call the Earned Income Tax Credit or other refundable credits a "tip credit." Those credits belong to the employee, not the employer, and have nothing to do with Form 8846.

What the FICA Tip Credit Actually Is

Employers pay 7.65% employer FICA on reported tips. Section 45B lets qualifying food and beverage employers claim a credit for that tax on the portion of tips that exceeds a $5.15 hourly floor. The credit is nonrefundable, claimed on Form 8846, and carried to Form 3800. It does not reduce the deposits you make with Form 941. Full details are on the FICA tip credit guide.

What the Wage-Law Tip Credit Is

The wage-law tip credit is the rule that lets an employer pay a cash wage as low as $2.13 (federal) or a higher state floor and still meet the minimum-wage obligation as long as tips make up the difference. It is enforced by the Department of Labor and state labor agencies. It has no Form 8846 component and does not appear on any income-tax return. State variations are summarized on the tip credit rules by state page.

When You Need a CPA

A single-location restaurant with clean tip reports can often prepare Form 8846 from payroll data. Multi-unit operators, partnerships with pass-through credits on Schedule K-1, and any return that also involves ERC, WOTC, or amended Form 941-X filings should involve a tax professional. The Form 8846 instructions page shows the line-by-line mechanics; a CPA decides how those numbers interact with the rest of the return.

FAQ

Can I claim the FICA tip credit and a state tip credit in the same year?

Yes. The wage-law tip credit (state or federal) and the FICA tip credit operate at different levels of government and are not mutually exclusive. One affects the cash wage you pay; the other is an income-tax credit.

Does Form 8846 reduce what I deposit with Form 941?

No. You continue to deposit and report employer FICA on tips in the ordinary way. The credit is claimed later on the income-tax return.

Is the FICA tip credit the same as a payroll-tax refund?

No. It is a nonrefundable income-tax credit. It cannot generate a cash refund by itself and does not appear on Form 941.
